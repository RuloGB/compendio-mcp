# Design: `read_doc` Returns a Section Outline for Large Documents

**Phase**: design, **revision 1 (2026-09-13)** · **Artifact store**: openspec · **Skill resolution**: fallback-registry (`cognitive-doc-design`)

## Revision 1: read this first

The stress probe (`stress-probe.mjs`) falsified revision 0 at scale. The rendered outline is 65%–117% of the document it replaces, and repeated H3 titles produce hundreds of rows that each return the whole document. At 5000 headings, `read_doc` runs out of JS heap and takes the MCP server down with it.

| Shape | Headings | Doc tokens | Outline tokens | Exec ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| apiRef | 105 / 1000 / 5000 | 6350 / 56304 / 280304 | 4133 / 36469 / 180850 | 2.5 / 175 / 4065 |
| flat | 500 / 5000 | 27974 / 280974 | 17983 / 180861 | 7 / 518 |
| changelog | 500 / 2000 / 5000 | 27865 / 111365 / — | 32624 / 130749 / OOM | 47 / 475 / OOM |

Root causes, verified in `buildOutline` and `formatOutlineSection`:

1. Every row repeats a 70–180-character annotation, and nothing bounds the row count.
2. `sectionMatcher` ignores ancestry. `### Added` matches every chunk that holds that heading line, so each of the 500 changelog H3 rows resolves to ~27,861 tokens. D9 collapses only within one bucket.
3. `ComputedRow.content` keeps the joined matched text for every row (O(rows × doc)). On top of that, `overlaps()` does an O(rows²) substring scan. The raw-line test also flags `1.0.0` because its own `### Added` line is byte-identical to another row's.

**What changes**: R1–R6 below. They replace D8's rendering, D9 (both halves) and the "outline size is unbounded" non-guarantee. **What stays**: D1–D7, the 6000-token threshold, the "2+ addressable" gate, round-trip of every listed row, the `HEADING_LINE` `\r?`, fence awareness, the `section` lookup contract (unchanged), the rule of no new `isFenceDelimiter` importer, and the hexagonal boundaries.

## Technical Approach

All logic stays in `src/application/read-document.ts` (D5). With no `section`, the document's size is measured as it is today. Above 6000 tokens, one pass over the chunks, **walked in `position` order** (the same order the occurrence table, the matcher's internal arrays, and R3's ownership walk all use — one pinned order, never store order), builds an **occurrence table** (every H2/H3 heading line with its level, title, normalized title, chunk index, and a global occurrence ordinal `k`). The candidate rows come from that table, one row per distinct normalized title (R1). The gate then counts addressable rows lazily. A deterministic budget ladder (R4) chooses which rows to render, and sizes and flags are computed **only for rendered rows**, from chunk lengths and chunk indices (R2, R3, R5). No joined per-row text is ever built.

| Decision | Choice | Status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 Row source | One level-aware scanner `headingLinesIn`. `headingsIn` is derived from it. The outline keeps H2/H3 only | Kept. It now feeds R1's occurrence table. Top-level orphan H3s and H2s share one namespace (R1) |

### Revision 1 decisions

| # | Choice | Rejected | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 One row per normalized title | Two passes over the occurrence table, both over the same `position`-ordered walk used everywhere else (Technical Approach). **Pass 1 (classify):** for every distinct normalized title, decide once whether it is a top-level row (some occurrence is an H2, or an H3 before any H2), a child row of one specific parent title (every occurrence sits under the same parent title), or `repeated` (an H3 occurs under ≥ 2 different parent titles). **Pass 2 (assemble):** each parent's `children` list is built from only the titles classified as "child of this parent" in pass 1 — a title promoted to top-level or to `repeated` in pass 1 is excluded from every parent's children, so it is never listed twice. A top-level row is positioned at its **first top-level occurrence**, which is not necessarily the title's true first occurrence in the document: a title that occurs first as an H3 child (e.g. at position 5) and later as an H2 (e.g. at position 20) is classified top-level (pass 1) and rendered at position 20, not 5 — its earlier, non-top-level occurrence contributes only to `occurrences` and to the pooled children, never to row position. The children of all of a top-level title's H2 occurrences are pooled in first-occurrence order. This also covers the single-parent case: a title with one top-level (H2, or orphan-H3-before-any-H2) occurrence and one H3 occurrence under a single H2 parent is a top-level row, and its H3 occurrence is pooled into that row's `occurrences` count and excluded from that parent's `children`, consistent with the cross-level promotion rule. A `repeated` entry is positioned at its first occurrence (of any level). Every row carries `occurrences` (its H2/H3 line count, document-wide). **Invariant: no two listed rows share a normalized title**. **Precedence when a title qualifies for both top-level and `repeated`:** a title can satisfy both classifications at once — e.g. it occurs as an H3 under ≥ 2 different H2 parents (the `repeated` condition) **and** also occurs at least once as an H2, or as an orphan H3 before any H2 (the top-level condition). Top-level wins. This is consistent with the cross-level promotion rule already stated above (a title with an early child occurrence and a later top-level occurrence is promoted to top-level, not left as a child); here it means a title otherwise eligible for `repeated` is promoted to top-level, and none of its occurrences are counted toward the `repeated` group, if pass 1 also finds a qualifying top-level occurrence. *Worked example:* a generic title `"Notes"` occurs as `### Notes` under H2 parent P1, as `### Notes` under a different H2 parent P2 (satisfying `repeated`), and once more as a plain `## Notes` elsewhere in the document (satisfying top-level). Pass 1 classifies `"Notes"` as top-level (positioned at its first *top-level* occurrence, i.e. that `## Notes`, per the position rule above); its two H3 occurrences under P1 and P2 pool into that same top-level row's `occurrences` count instead of forming a separate `repeated` entry | (a) Addressing each copy as `"Parent > Child"`: does not resolve when the parent was not descended (Findings row 2), which is the changelog case. (b) Dropping repeated titles entirely: hides a real request ("all Added sections") that costs one row. (c) A single pass that both classifies and assembles: cannot know a title is top-level until its last occurrence is seen, so an eager single-pass assembly can wrongly leave an early occurrence under a parent before the later top-level occurrence is found | The matcher is title-keyed, so the title is the only unit that can be requested. One row per title makes each listed row a distinct request. Changelog/500 goes from ~500 useless H3 rows to 3 |
| R2 Short flags + legend | Row line `{indent}- {heading} (~{tokens}[, xN][, too large][, +other])`. A `Flags:` line explains each flag **once**, and only for flags that occur | Keeping the prose annotations | The prose accounts for most of the 65–117% ratio. The flags keep D8's instruction (narrower heading, or own file tool) and cost ≤ 19 characters per row |
| R3 `+other` = response includes text outside the heading's own sections | Occurrences carry a single global ordinal `k`, assigned by walking chunks in `position` order and, within one chunk, by the order `headingLinesIn` finds its heading lines (`IndexedChunk` has no line number, so `k` — not a source line — is the unit of ownership; see worked examples below). A heading occurrence's **own sections** run from its occurrence `k` up to `end(k)`, the next occurrence whose heading level number is ≤ `k`'s level — i.e. the same level or a shallower depth (or the end of the walk if none exists). For a chunk C: `firstOwner(C)` is C's own first heading-line occurrence if C contains at least one, else the nearest occurrence at or before C in the walk (from an earlier chunk). **A chunk that precedes every occurrence in the walk** — the intro chunk before the document's first H2/H3, or any other chunk that happens to sit before the first occurrence — has no such occurrence to inherit at all: `firstOwner(C)` and `lastOwner(C)` are **undefined** for it, and an undefined owner satisfies no row's interval, ever (worked example (d)). `lastOwner(C)` is C's own last heading-line occurrence if C contains at least one, else the same inherited (or undefined) value as `firstOwner(C)`. This is how a **zero-heading continuation chunk** (an oversized childless H2 split by `splitToBound`, where only the first physical piece keeps the literal `## Heading` line) and a **multi-occurrence chunk** (several H2/H3 lines sharing one physical chunk — either an H2 with its inline H3 children, fused by `chunkOutline`'s non-descend rule, or 2+ distinct H2 pieces fused by `mergeTinyPieces`) are both handled without a source line number: the former inherits its single owner from the previous chunk; the latter simply has more than one occurrence whose `firstOwner`/`lastOwner` fall inside the same chunk.<br><br>**Single-occurrence chunk containment.** A chunk is inside a single occurrence's interval `[k, end(k))` when both `firstOwner(C)` and `lastOwner(C)` fall in that range.<br><br>**Multi-occurrence row containment (generalized).** A row backed by more than one occurrence of its normalized title — pooled identical-title H2s and same-parent identical-title H3s (R1's collapse, kept tasks 4.1/4.2), or a `repeated` entry pooling an H3's occurrences across different parents (R1) — has its **own sections** defined as the **union** of `[k_i, end(k_i))` over every occurrence `k_i` it pools. A matched chunk C is inside the row's own sections iff **some** pooled `k_i` puts C inside `[k_i, end(k_i))` (an undefined `firstOwner`/`lastOwner` never satisfies this, for any `k_i`); the flag is set only when a matched chunk is inside **none** of the pooled intervals. A single-occurrence row is the special case where the union has exactly one member, so the rule above still applies unchanged to worked examples (a) and (b). Computed with a stack in O(L) over the occurrence table (L = total occurrences, not source lines), plus O(\|M\| · log L) per rendered row.<br><br>**Consequence (a) — why kept tasks 4.1/4.2 and pooled identical H2s stay unflagged.** For a pooled occurrence `k_i` whose own owning chunk contains no *earlier* occurrence fused ahead of it (no foreign content merged in before it — true for pooled H2s, and for same-parent H3s only when that parent descends so each H3 has its own chunk), that chunk's `firstOwner` is `k_i` itself, which trivially lies in `[k_i, end(k_i))`. ## Guarantees and non-guarantees (updated)

- **Kept**: every listed row, including `repeated` entries and rows shown after truncation, round-trips through `section`. CRLF titles carry no `\r`. Headings inside balanced fences are never listed. Setext headings are not detected. Frontmatter is excluded from the threshold check. `addressable` is never serialized.
- **Kept**: two adjacent sections fused at index time are both listed, with the same combined size, and both are flagged `+other`.
- **Removed (falsified)**: "the outline's own size is unbounded in principle but measured small in practice".
- **New**: the rows block is at most 8000 characters by construction. The fixed text (header, legend, notice, group label) is at most about 1000 characters plus the path. Frontmatter is not budgeted, same as the `document` variant.
- **New non-guarantees**: a row whose own line exceeds the budget is never shown. After truncation, fewer than 2 addressable rows may be visible; the gate is decided over at most the first `MAX_GATE_CANDIDATES = 2000` candidates in the pinned order (R4), not necessarily every candidate — a document with ≥ 2000 candidates where the first 2000 include fewer than 2 addressable rows falls back to `document` even if a later candidate would have qualified. Within that cap, gate cost is bounded, not quadratic: O(2000 × C) worst case, replacing the earlier unbounded "grows quadratically" statement.

## Data Flow

    execute({path}) -> chunks -> length(content)        (no string built yet)
      <= 6000 or chunks.length == 1          -> document (build the string now)
      scan once, position order -> occurrence table (level, title, norm, chunk index, ordinal k, owner/end)
      R1 two-pass group by norm -> top rows + children + repeated group
      D4 lazy gate over candidates (stop at 2 addressable, or after 2000 evaluated) -> < 2 -> document
      R4 ladder on pessimistic costs -> rendered set + omitted
      R5/R3 for rendered rows only: matchIndices -> tokens (lengths), +other (intervals)
      -> { type: "outline", meta, tokens, sections, repeated, omitted }

## Interfaces / Contracts

```ts
export interface OutlineSection {
  heading: string;               // verbatim `section` value
  tokens: number;                // D2, computed from chunk lengths
  occurrences: number;           // H2/H3 lines sharing this normalized title (>= 1); the row's `xN` clause (R2) renders iff occurrences > 1
  oversized: boolean;            // tokens > OUTLINE_THRESHOLD_TOKENS
  includesOtherContent: boolean; // R3 (replaces overlapsOtherSection)
  children: OutlineSection[];    // [] for H3 rows and `repeated` entries
}
export type OutlineOmission =
  | { kind: "none" }
  | { kind: "subheadings"; hidden: number }
  | { kind: "truncated"; shown: number; total: number };
// ReadResult member:
| { type: "outline"; meta: DocumentMeta; tokens: number; sections: OutlineSection[];
    repeated: OutlineSection[]; omitted: OutlineOmission }
export const OUTLINE_ROWS_BUDGET_CHARS = 8000;
export function formatOutlineRow(row: Omit<OutlineSection, "children">, depth: 0 | 1): string;
```

A `repeated` entry never reaches the rendered group while an `omitted` notice applies. Numbers are rendered with `String(n)`. Example literal output, full level (changelog shape):

```
---
status: active
---

Document "docs/CHANGELOG.md" is too large to return whole (~27865 tokens; limit 6000).
Call read_doc again with one of these headings, verbatim, as section. (~N) is the estimated size in tokens of that response.
Flags: "xN" = the heading occurs N times and the response contains all of them; "too large" = still over 6000 tokens, request a narrower heading or use your own file-reading tool; "+other" = the response also contains text from other headings.
- 1.0.0 (~165)
- 1.1.0 (~165)
Repeated under several headings (listed once):
- Added (~27861, x167, too large, +other)
```

(`Added`'s `+other` here follows R3's generalized multi-occurrence rule: it comes from `Fixed`/`Changed`/version-heading text fused into the same chunk as each pooled `Added` occurrence, not from the row pooling 167 occurrences — see R3's consequence (b) and worked example (a). A `repeated` row with the same occurrence count but no such fused foreign content would render without `+other`.)

Flag clauses appear in the fixed order `xN`, `too large`, `+other`. On a given row, `xN` renders iff that row's `occurrences > 1`; `too large` and `+other` each render iff the row's own boolean is true. The `Flags:` legend line explains a flag only when some *listed* row uses it. The notice lines go after the `Flags:` line:

- `subheadings`: `{hidden} subheadings and repeated headings are not listed (outline limit). Request a listed heading, or find a subsection with search_docs and pass its section value.` — `hidden` is the sum of the omitted children rows and the omitted `repeated` group entries (R4 step 2 drops both).
- `truncated`: `Only the first {shown} of {total} top-level headings are listed, without subheadings (outline limit). For the others, find the section with search_docs and pass its section value, or use your own file-reading tool.`

**Unchanged from revision 0 and already implemented**: the `read_doc` description sentence, the `section` parameter's "or a heading from read_doc's outline", and the `SERVER_INSTRUCTIONS` before/after, with their `test/server.test.ts` assertions.
